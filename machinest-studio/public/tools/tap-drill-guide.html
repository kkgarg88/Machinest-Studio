Hole preparation guide

Through hole

  • Confirm workpiece thickness and required full thread length.
  • Provide clearance for tap entry and exit.
  • Plan chip evacuation and suitable cutting fluid for the material.
  • Check whether the tap is a spiral-point, spiral-flute or form tap.

Blind hole

  • Drill deeper than the required full thread; the drill point and tap lead consume depth.
  • Allow bottom clearance so the tap does not bottom out.
  • Consider a spiral-flute tap for chip evacuation, where suitable.
  • Verify drill point angle and tap lead length from tool data.
Safety / accuracy: Hole-depth allowance depends on drill point angle, tap chamfer/lead, tap style, material and the required full thread. This guide does not calculate a universal blind-hole drill depth. Verify critical threads with the tap supplier and drawing requirements.

Roll (form) tap guide

A roll tap makes the thread by pushing the metal aside instead of cutting it, so there are no chips.

Where roll taps work well

  • Ductile materials: aluminium alloys, brass, copper, low-carbon and alloy steels, austenitic stainless.
  • Blind holes, because there are no chips to pack at the bottom.
  • Threads that need extra strength: the formed thread is work-hardened and has a smooth surface.

Where not to use them

  • Cast iron and other brittle materials.
  • High-silicon aluminium castings and very hard or hardened steel.
  • Check the tap maker's hardness and strength limit for the material you are running.

Pre-hole drill

  • Use the Roll tap drill value from the calculator or table. It is larger than the cutting-tap drill.
  • The hole size sets the thread depth. Too small gives high torque and tap breakage; too large gives a weak, short thread.
  • Keep the hole size tight, about ±0.05 mm or better, with a sharp drill.

Machine and lubrication

  • Roll taps need more torque than cutting taps. Use rigid tapping or a good tension-compression holder.
  • Use neat oil or a forming lubricant. Thin coolant gives high torque and a poor thread.
  • Follow the tap maker's speed chart for the material.

Checking the thread

  • Check with go / no-go thread gauges.
  • A small raised ridge can form at the hole entry. A light chamfer or countersink removes it and helps the gauge start.
  • Run a test hole before starting a batch.

Cutting tap or roll tap?

  • Cutting tap: any material, simple, lower torque, makes chips.
  • Roll tap: ductile material only, no chips, stronger thread, needs the exact pre-hole size and good lubrication.
Accuracy: The roll-tap drill sizes here are calculated for about 70% thread and are a starting point. Different tap makers and materials use different values, so confirm with your tap supplier's chart for critical threads.
