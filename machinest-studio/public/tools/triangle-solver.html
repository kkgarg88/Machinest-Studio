      Shop examples

      Common machining jobs that are really right-angle triangle problems.

      Chamfer: length from angle

      • Chamfer 2 mm × 30° on the face side. Base b = 2, angle A = 30°.
      • Height a = 2 × tan 30° = 1.155 mm (the X or diameter-side move is double: 2.309 on diameter).
      • Fill b = 2 and A = 30 in the right-angle tab.

      Taper: angle from two diameters

      • Ø40 to Ø30 over 50 mm length. Radius change a = (40 − 30) ÷ 2 = 5, length b = 50.
      • Half angle A = atan(5 ÷ 50) = 5.711°. Included taper angle is double: 11.421°.
      • Fill a = 5 and b = 50.

      Countersink depth

      • 90° countersink to Ø12 on a Ø6.6 hole. Radius step a = (12 − 6.6) ÷ 2 = 2.7. Base b runs along the hole axis, so the half angle 45° is angle A.
      • Depth b = a ÷ tan 45° = 2.7 mm from the hole edge. For a full-point depth, use a = 6 (Ø12 ÷ 2).
      • Fill a = 2.7 and A = 45.

      Drill point length

      • Ø10 drill with 118° point. a = 5 (radius), base b along the drill axis, half point angle A = 59°.
      • Point length b = 5 ÷ tan 59° = 3.004 mm. Add this to the full-diameter depth.
      • Fill a = 5 and A = 59.

      Angled hole or slot position

      • A hole 40 mm along a line at 25° from the X axis. Hypotenuse c = 40, angle A = 25°.
      • X = b = 40 × cos 25° = 36.252, Y = a = 40 × sin 25° = 16.905.
      • Fill c = 40 and A = 25.

      Any triangle uses

      • Three hole centres with known distances: enter the 3 distances to get the angles between them.
      • Two sides and the angle between them: get the third distance (law of cosines).
      • If two answers are possible (two sides and an angle not between them), both are shown.
      Tip: on a lathe, X is on diameter. Anything you get for side a as a radius must be doubled for the X move.
